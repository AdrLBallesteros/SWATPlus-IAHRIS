## ***WHY EVALUATE THE ALTERATION OF THE NATURAL FLOW REGIME?***

This question can be answered by considering three points of view: legal, scientific and managerial.

From the **legal** point of view, it is necessary to assess the alteration of the flow regime because this is established by the Water Framework Directive (WFD). This directive sets as a priority objective that the management of water resources must achieve good ecological status. In order to achieve this objective, it is necessary to have protocols that allow an objective and efficient knowledge of the ecological status of rivers, and the WFD, in its annex V, contains a series of components of the river ecosystem that must necessarily be considered in order to assess their ecological status, including the hydrological regime.

From the <strong>scientific</strong> point of view, the transcendence of flow regime as an articulator and vertebrator element of the river ecosystem has had a relatively recent recognition, reaching its zenith with the formulation of the paradigm of natural flow regime: <em>"the full range of intra- and inter-annual variation of hydrological regime with its associated characteristics of magnitude, seasonality, duration, frequency and rate of change, are critical to sustain natural biodiversity and the integrity of aquatic ecosystems"</em> (Poff <em>et al</em>., 1997). The corollary of this axiom is immediate: success in the conservation of the biodiversity and functionality of our rivers depends on our ability to know, protect and/or restore the main aspects of natural flow regime.

For the river <strong>management</strong> authorities, it is necessary to know in what situation the aspects of the flow regime only from this knowledge is it possible to formulate appropriate diagnoses on which to base policies of use and management that allow to advance towards the objective of "<em>good ecological status</em>"or the optimal "potential." water bodies which may be qualified as "<em>highly hydrologically altered</em>", it is necessary to characterize both the current situation of the flow regime and the optimal hydrological potential compatible with the conditions that require that this condition be assigned.

## ***WHAT IS IAHRIS?***

IAHRIS (**I**ndices of **A**lteration **H**ydrologic in **Ri**ver**S**) is a **computer application** used to obtain:

- **parameters** with which to **characterize the hydrological regime**, both natural and altered, at a point in a river. These parameters evaluate hydrological aspects of the regime with marked environmental significance (duration of the droughts, seasonality of the floods… ).

- If the user provides data of the two regimes, natural and altered, the application also calculates **indices** that **assess the degree of alteration of the hydrological regime** in those aspects of greater environmental significance.

- The application also provides the environmental interpretation of the alteration previously evaluated, summarizing its significance on the main components of the affected ecosystem: morphology, fish fauna, benthic macroinvertebrates, fluvial habitat and riparian vegetation.

- In the above circumstances, i.e. having data on a natural and altered basis, the application assigns the **condition of water body "highly hydrologically altered"** on two complementary criteria (P10-90 and IAH-MMA criteria).

- For a given natural regime, the application can obtain a **set of minimum ecological flow scenarios** specified at the level of monthly contribution.

- IAHRIS also offers a tool to assess the environmental suitability of different ecological flow regime in its component of minimum flow, as well as the adequacy of the implementation of those scenarios.

- Finally, the application allows the download of the monthly contributions of the SIMPA model at the OAS capacity stations or at any other point specified by the user.

**RECALLS:**

- **parameter:** Each of the variables that, numerically, makes it possible to characterize the aspects of greater environmental significance of the flow regime.
- **index:** Relationship between the values of the same parameter, in natural regime and in altered regime. It makes it possible to quantify the differences between the reference state – natural regime – and any other – altered regime.

## ***WHAT CRITERIA HAVE BEEN USED TO SELECT THE PARAMETERS?***

The paradigm of flow regime already indicates the main aspects of the flow regime The link between these aspects of the regime and key factors of the river ecosystem can be summarized as follows:

- <strong>Magnitude</strong>: since it determines the <em><strong>availability</strong></em> general water in the ecosystem.
- <strong>Frequency</strong> with which an event occurs in a given time interval: indicative of variability in the flow regime and conditioning of the geomorphological and ecological dynamics, and therefore of the <em><strong>diversity</strong></em>.
- <strong>Duration</strong> or time interval associated with certain flow conditions: in extreme situations, floods and droughts, the duration is closely linked to the thresholds of <em><strong>resilience</strong></em> of the different species.
- <strong>Seasonality</strong> or regularity with which that event occurs at a certain time of year: it is a closely linked aspect and <em><strong>synchrony</strong></em> with the life cycles of the species (fluvial, estuaries and marine).
- <strong>Exchange rates</strong>: on the speed with which changes take place from one magnitude to another, affecting the <em><strong>response capacity</strong></em> biota.

Therefore, the process of characterizing flow regime should consider these five aspects, and should do so taking into account both average or usual values, as well as determinants of the overall availability of water in the ecosystem, as well as extreme values –floods and droughts- in defining these the most critical conditions that the ecosystem endures, and considering both year-on-year and seasonal variability.

## ***HOW TO QUANTIFY THE ALTERATION OF THE NATURAL FLOW REGIME?***

The WFD states that the characterization of the ecological status should be considered by comparing the situation that is to be analyzed with the reference one. For each of the elements to consider –hydrological regime; macroinvertebrates; phytobenths; fish fauna...- it is due to:

1) Define the variables to be measured according to their ability to reflect the environmental integrity of the analyzed element.

2) Establish the reference state, i.e. the values that these variables would take for minimum conditions alteration of the river ecosystem.

3) Calculate the Ecological Quality Ratios (EQR) as a ratio between the value of the variable for the current conditions and that corresponding to it in the reference state.

Accepted that the natural flow regime is the factor that determines the integrity of the river ecosystem to a greater extent, and having tools –parameters- to quantify the most important environmental aspects of the regime, indices to assess objectively the extent to which the regime currently in operation, other than the natural regime – altered regime – or any other proposed regime – for example an environmental regime of flows- differs or resembles the natural, since that analogy or difference will determine the current or potential integrity of the river.

## ***WHAT INFORMATION IS NECESSARY?***

As a general rule, IAHRIS **recommends having at least 15 full years** of data to collect year-on-year variability of hydrological regimeThis ensures a minimum of information with which to obtain reasonably acceptable conclusions related to variability and extreme values.

However, the application allows the analysis of shorter series, provided that at least seven full years of records are available. When the availability is between 7 and 14 years, the type of study is called **REDUCED SERIES** and shall be detailed in Annex 2 to this document.

**IMPORTANT:**

- The recommended period of analysis is at least 15 full years of data in the natural and altered series.
- If one of the series to be analyzed has between 7 and 14 years, the study is also possible**,** but in this case it will be classified as **REDUCED.** The whole case of this situation will be developed in the **ANNEX 2** of this manual.

In either of the two cases cited (≥15 years or between 14 and 7 years), the data to be contained in the series may be daily flows (m<sup>3</sup>/s) or monthly contributions (hm<sup>3</sup>). If daily and monthly series of the same regime are provided and covering the same period of time, the application will only consider the information obtained from the reading of the flows It is therefore necessary to provide only series with daily flows and monthly contributions if the records cover different dates.

The data are grouped into two types:

- Series in regime **NATURAL**: contains records corresponding to the natural flow regime. At each point of analysis it is only possible to contribute to the implementation **maximum** of **two series**, one with monthly contributions and one with daily flows.
- Series in regime **ALTERNATIVE**: contains records corresponding to a regime other than that of nature.

(a) The use and/or regulation of the river.

(b) A simulation, resulting from the implementation of infrastructure management protocols, an environmental regime scenario flows or any other hypothesis derived from the hydrological planning of the system.

The application admits at each point of analysis as many altered regimes as the user wants to analyze, providing for each of these regimes a maximum of two series, one with monthly contributions and the other with daily flows.

The type of information provided to the implementation determines the results obtained. In particular, these results depend on the following aspects:

- periodicity of records: daily mean-flow data or monthly contributions
- series contemporaneity: whether or not the data of the natural and altered regimes that are compared are from the same period
- extension of the series: number of full years provided (≥15 years or between 14 and 7 years).

The application also offers a set of descriptive statistics of the series of annual contributions calculated from the data provided.

Thus the records of each month are classified in wet, average or dry according to whether their monthly contribution is in the quartile that corresponds to the highest values of that month -wet-, in the quartile of the lowest of that month -dry- or in the two intermediates -average-. The application also offers a set of descriptive statistics of the series of monthly contributions.

## ***WHAT DATA TYPES CAN BE COMPLETED?***

The application uses time series of data corresponding to <strong>daily flows</strong>, expressed in m<sup>3</sup>/s, and/or a <strong>monthly contributions</strong>, expressed in hm<sup>3</sup>. In both cases, each record –flow or contribution - must carry <strong>date associated with the corresponding date</strong>, day, month and year in the case of flow daily, and month and year for monthly contribution.

For each point of the river only up to two series can be available on a natural basis, one with flows and one with monthly contributions.

For example, in the case of a dam which has been in operation for years, it may be considered as a single series corresponding to the altered regime, or several, if over that period the management criteria have changed, so that each of the series flows For each of these periods it would be treated as an independent series, all with the character of altered regime and associated to the same point of the river. Another case for which several series in altered regime would be available for the same point of the river would be that in which different strategies of management of a hydraulic infrastructure were analyzed: for each hypothesis, and with the same or different time window, the corresponding simulated series would be generated, and each of these series would constitute a different altered regime linked to the same point of the river.

This possibility for the user to have at one point a river of more than one associated series in altered regime means that the application provides for a specific treatment for these series.

## ***APPLICATION AND THE ASSOCIATED DATABASE***

In order to properly understand the structure, data management and operation of the application, it is necessary to bear in mind that its core is the database.

There are three key concepts around which the application organizes and manages information, calculations and results:

- **DRAFT:** entity or unit of work comprising a set of points, as well as their associated alterations and series**.**
- **POINT:** Point means the section or section of river for which data are available; daily flows and/or monthly contributions, either in natural or altered regime or in both regimes. All points to be stored in the database require an Identifier CODE and a DESCRIPTION, as many points as you want can be entered, provided that your Identifier CODE is different from any other code used for another point in the same project.
- **SERIES:** Series of daily mean flows or monthly contributions associated with a particular POINT. All SERIES used by the application require to be assigned:

- **Type of periodicity:**  MONTHLY OR DAILY
- **Type of regime:** NATURAL OR ALTERNATIVE
- **PUNTO code associated with the natural regime**
- **Code of alteration associated** (only for series corresponding to an altered regime)

The maximum number of series associated with a certain point is 2\*(1+No associated alterations), since each point can have two different series associated (of monthly contributions and of daily mean flows) for each type of regime.

- <strong>alteration:</strong> As has already been pointed out in the first heading of this chapter, at one point several series may be considered corresponding to different altered regimes. In order to facilitate the management of this information, the application requires that any series of altered regime is always associated with a POINT and is declared in the database with an Identifier CODE of the alteration and a DESCRIPTION, can be introduced, for a certain POINT, as many altered regimes as you want, provided that the <u>ID code is different from any other used for an alteration within the same project</u>, regardless of the point to which the latter is associated.

The contemporaneity of the series in natural and altered regime is a very important characteristic that the user must always know before making an interpretation of the results. Ideally, the series submitted to the analysis would be contemporaneous; thus the alterations shown by the indices They would have no variability-related components that could present the non-common time periods. An alternative is to require contemporaneity to calculate the indices, but this option could leave out the analysis of cases of interest, even if running the risk of "polluting" the indices With the "noise" of comparing behaviors in different periods of time. ## ***HOW DO DATA CHARACTERISTICS IN THE RESULTS OF IAHRIS?***

The series can be characterized by three aspects: periodicity, nature and contemporaneity. Below, it is presented, in a summary way, how these characteristics determine the results offered by the application.

<strong>PERIODICITY:</strong> They may contain two types of records: daily mean flows (m<sup>3</sup>/s) or monthly contributions (hm<sup>3</sup>). Each series can only contain data of the same type.

How does periodicity influence results?

Having only a series of monthly contributions limits the results to three parameters –P1; P2 and P3-, and five indices –IAH1; IAH2; IAH4; IAH 5 and IAH6- linked, parameters and indices, only to the component of Habitual Values. The characterization of floods and/or droughts requires daily records. The global index for highly altered water bodies shall be carried out solely on the basis of available indices.

**NATURE:** The records, whether daily or monthly, may correspond to the natural regime or to an altered regime. For a point of study, the natural regime is unique, although up to two series with data of this nature can be available, one with daily records and another with monthly records. As many altered regimes as the user has information can be considered. At least 7 years of records are indispensable, not necessarily consecutive, although as mentioned above it is recommended to work with at least 15 years.

How does the nature of the regime influence the results?

The characterization of natural and altered regimes is independent. That is, we can characterize the natural regime without having an associated altered regime and vice versa.

However, for the evaluation of the alteration, it is essential to have the records of both regimes, whether contemporaneous or not and always of at least 7 years.

**CONTEMPORANEITY:** Common quality of the natural and altered series of a point referring to the fact that the information contained in those series corresponds to the same years.

How does contemporaneity influence results?

The ideal situation, thus qualified because it would allow IAHRIS to offer the most complete information, corresponds to the case in which the user has natural and altered contemporaneous series of fifteen years or more. For that case, the application would facilitate for each of the regimes the 19 parameters (Table No 1), as well as 21 alteration indices (Table No2) plus all three alteration global indices (alteration index of usual values; alteration index of floods; alteration index of droughts).

However, when the natural and altered series are less than fifteen years old, or having more the user decides not to consider that condition (in IAHRIS, and for the two circumstances, the case is identified with <strong>NON-CONTEMPORANEITY</strong>), the application uses <strong>parameters</strong> and <strong>indices</strong> specific to that case (see <em>Methodological Reference Manual</em>).

The index of highly altered water bodies will be obtained from the indices calculated in each case.

IAHRIS is presented as a support system for the decision in the selection of the ecological regime of flows Minimum (RCEmin)

## ***WHAT DATA ARE NECESSARY?***

- **For the generation of RCEmin scenarios and the assessment of their hydro-environmental quality**

The module of ecological flows necessarily requires data on the natural regime of <u>at least 15 full years</u> (daily or monthly).

The justification for this requirement is that the RCEmin scenarios calculated by the application are as discussed, hydrologically based, and use the natural regime as a reference for their estimation. If the availability of data is less than the quoted value, the module does not apply and the user receives a notice when attempting to execute it.

As an additional restriction, the module cannot be applied when the natural regime has a temporary, intermittent or ephemeral character, since the methodologies used are not suitable for regimes with this type of nature. To verify this circumstance the application performs a previous check of the data provided in natural regime consisting of the following:

*The natural regime data calculates the 80% and 90% exceedance percentages (P80 and P90 respectively) for each month and checks:*

*A) Does the natural regime have two or more months with P90 = 0?*

*B) Does the natural regime present one or more months with P80=0?*

If the answer to either option is YES, the methodology is NOT applied and the user receives a notice when trying to run the module.

Therefore, when a natural series of at least 15 full years of data is available and not affected by temporality, the application calculates -at monthly level - 11 scenarios of hydrologically based minimum ecological-flow scenarios.

These scenarios are:

- Tennant (1976)
- Tessman (1980)
- VMF, Variability monthly flow (2016)
- NMTM, New Modified Tennant Method (2014), with 4 scenarios: minimum or P90, Fair, Good and Excellent
- ADMM, 2024 Modified Version of Annual Distribution Method (2013)
- IAHRIS\_CER (2024) with three scenarios IAHRIS\_P90, IAHRIS\_P85 and IAHRIS\_P80

It then conducts the assessment of its environmental quality through three parameters: score, environmental demand and efficiency:

- Score: it is a quantitative assessment -set between 100 and -100) of the RCEmin scenario. It turns out to be compared with high percentages of excess of the natural regime, considering magnitude, seasonality and variability
- Environmental demand: is the ratio between the annual contribution required by the CERmin scenario and the average annual contribution of the natural regime.
- Efficiency: is the ratio between the Score and the annual contribution required by the RCmin scenario. Its value indicates the number of points achieved with each hm<sup>3</sup> CERmin.

For more detailed information, please refer to the Methodological Reference Manual for estimating and evaluating minimum ecological flows

- The Mass Report will not be presented highly hydrologically altered (Inf 8).

The selector of "*Include subdirectories*" allows the application to search for CSV files inside the subfolders of the specified directory, not just the files in the root directory.

The button "*Process Directory*" runs the application processing and shows us a list of the points and alterations that will be added to IAHRIS in the CSV information area.

**CSV Information Area**

This area shows us the name of each CSV file read, the point name and the alteration if you have read from each CSV and if the CSV has been processed or not, and if it has not been processed shows us the error by which it has not been. This application does not allow to edit or modify any of the CSV read so if an error appears we must modify it directly in the CSV and reload the directory.

**Processing Summary Area**

Here we will see the number of CSV files processed, the number of points and alterations that contain those CSV and the number of wrong CSV files there. The button "*Save Report"* allows us to save the CSV information table in Excel format.

**Processing Execution Area**

We have two buttons: "*Delete All*" allows us to reset the interface and reload a directory and "*Generate File*" A search engine will open us first to indicate where and by what name we want to save the .bat file.

You can save the .bat file in any path but it is recommended to save it in the folder where the CSVs are located or in the output of the reports to have the file located in case you need to run it again or edit it at some time.

**Export Area**

Here is the name of the Project that will be created in IAHRIS and where all points and alterations will be saved.The user can add a brief description of the project or leave it blank.

If the name of the project already exists in IAHRIS, the points and alterations will be created and the CSV will be loaded into that existing project. If it does not exist, one will be created and it will appear that it has been generated automatically by the application.

**Reporting Settings Area**

The selector "*Export Report*" allows you to select whether or not to export Excel reports from processed points and alterations. If we mark it, it is enabled to select the path where we want the reports to be saved.

*CONTEMPORANEITY SELECTORS*

- These selectors are equivalent to those found in IAHRIS and allow to select the contemporaneous option for the calculation of indices of usual values o/y floods and droughts.
- Activating these options means that IAHRIS will only work with those data corresponding to contemporaneous records in both regimes. It is recommended to always be enabled because this option removes the "noise" involved in working with non-contemporaneous data.
- When working in batches you cannot select individually for each point, thus being a common option to all points of the project.
- These selectors will only be applied if the data allows. For example, if at one of the processed points the data are not contemporaneous, IAHRIS will ignore the contemporaneity requirement for that point by generating the corresponding reports. Of this "decision" IAHRIS will account in the error output.

The contemporaneity of the data can also be consulted in the caratula of the reports generated by the utility.

**Save Area**

Two buttons available, "s*olo save bat*"; "*save and run bat*".

The save button only creates the bat file in the selected path allowing the user to execute it when they want to or edit it manually before running it. The save and run button saves the file in the desired path and executes it instantly.

<strong><u>IAHRIS.EXE COMMAND LINE</u></strong>

IAHRIS allows the execution of different commands by command line in addition to its graphical interface.

As a rule, the parameters of the command line must be passed between double quotation marks ("parameter") to prevent special characters such as spaces from causing unwanted interpretations of such commands.

The parameters can be passed in any order after indicating the operation to be performed.

The character ":" (two points) is a character not allowed in the names and descriptions. It can only be used in the paths to indicate the unit. Example: "C:\Users"

Generic syntax is IAHRIS.exe [Operations] [parameters]

**CD – Data Load**

This operation allows you to create a point or an alteration If the project does not exist it will create one with the name indicated. The point can be created empty (without loading data from a csv) or indicate a path of a csv file to load that data.

**Data Load of a Point**

parameters

/t:P Here we indicate that we are going to perform the operation, in this case a point.

/np:"" The name we want to give to the point. Limited to 12 characters.

/d:"" Description for the point. Limited to 20 characters.

/p:"" Name of the project where we want to add the point. Limited to 20 characters.

/dp:"" Description for the project.

/rn:"" Name of the natural regime that we want to assign to the point.

/ra:""Abbreviation of the natural regime that we want to assign to the point.

/fe:"" Route of the csv we want to load at the point. If we do not want to load data we must indicate it with empty quotes.

Example of syntax:

CD /t:P /np:"TIPO\_2" /d:"Point Type 2" /p:"PointsPro" /dp:"Points Project" /rn:"Natural" /ra:"nat"

/fe:"C:\Users\jperera1\Desktop\Points\NATURALREGIME\_ SIMPA\_3014.csv"

**Data Load of an alteration**

parameters

/t:A Here we indicate that we are going to perform the operation, in this case an alteration.

/np:""The name of the point associated with the alteration. MUST EXIST, if you will not give us an error indicating that we must create the point

first. Limited to 12 characters.

/na:""The name we want to give to the alteration. Limited to 12 characters.

/d:"" Description for the alteration. Limited to 20 characters.

/p:"" Name of the project where we want to add the alteration. Limited to 20 characters.

/dp:"" Description for the project.

/rn:"" Name of the natural regime that we want to assign to the point.

/ra:""Abbreviation of the natural regime that we want to assign to the point.

/fe:"" Route of the csv we want to load on the alteration. If we do not want to load data we must indicate it with empty quotation marks.

Example of syntax:

IAHRIS.exe CD /t:A /np:"TIPO\_6A" /na:"6A\_ALT" /d: "Alter 6"

/p:"PuntosPro" /dp:"Puntos Proyecto" /rn:"Alterado" /ra:"alt"

/fe:"C:\Users\jperera1\Desktop\Points\Cabriel\_altered\_MONTHUAL.csv"

GIS – Generate Output Report

This operation exports the IAHRIS report for a point or alteration in the selected project.

**Generate Point Output Report**

parameters

/t:P Here we indicate that we are going to perform the operation, in this case a point.

/np:""The name of the point from which we want to export the report.

/p:"" Name of the project to which the point from which we will export the report belongs

/fs:"" Path where we want to export the report.

-n	optional parameter indicating if you add that you do not overwrite in case you find a report with the same name to which you are going to

export.

Example of syntax:

IAHRIS.exe GIS /t:P /np:"TIPO\_2" /p:"PointsPro"

/fs:"C:\Users\jperera1\Desktop\Export" -n

**Generate Output Report from an alteration**

parameters

/t:P Here we indicate that we are going to perform the operation, in this case a point.

/np:""" The name of the point to which the alteration from which we will export the report.

/na:""The name of the alteration from which we want to export the report.

/p:"" Name of the project to which the point and the alteration from which we will export the report

/fs:"" Path where we want to export the report.

-n	optional parameter indicating if you add that you do not overwrite in case you find a report with the same name to which you are going to

export.

of what value has been used.

-cas.	optional parameter indicating that contemporaneity will be used

floods The console will inform us what value has been used.

Example of syntax:

IAHRIS.exe GIS /t:A /np:"TIPO\_6A" /na:"6A\_ALT" /p:"PointsPro"

/fs:"C:\Users\jperera1\Desktop\Export" -n -cvh –cas
